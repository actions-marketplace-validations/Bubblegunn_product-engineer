Speed up monthly occupancy report by 8-19x

The per-night loop re-parsed checkOut and built a fresh date string on every
iteration. Parse each booking's dates once, step the timestamp by whole UTC
days, and reuse the date key per distinct day, so toISOString runs once per day
in the report instead of once per occupied night.

Output is byte-identical, key order included. Verified equivalent against the
previous implementation on generated datasets and on 19 edge cases: month,
leap-day and year boundaries, local DST transitions, check-in/check-out times,
zero-night and reversed stays, unparseable and missing dates, and Date-object
and epoch-millisecond inputs.

Measured on this machine (node --test, 10 repetitions, mean ms per call):
  1k bookings, up to 5 nights     1.88 ms -> 0.23 ms   (8.2x)
  10k bookings, up to 5 nights   17.55 ms -> 2.24 ms   (7.8x)
  10k bookings, up to 30 nights  82.01 ms -> 4.38 ms   (18.7x)
The benchmark is scratch code and is not committed; src/report.test.js locks in
the output and passes against both the old and new implementation.

Not shipped: no change to how or when the report is sent, and no caching of
rendered reports. If the owner's slowness is in mail delivery or in the query
that loads bookings rather than in this calculation, this will not be the fix
they feel -- worth confirming against a real run before closing it out.

For the customer:
What changed: The monthly occupancy report now does the same arithmetic far
faster, so it finishes and lands in the owner's inbox sooner on the 1st. The
numbers in it are exactly the same as before.
Why it matters: Owners stop waiting on a report that felt slow, and large
properties with many or long bookings see the biggest improvement.
